How to read these numbers

Fill rate is not applicant match rate. It describes how many available positions were filled—not the chance that any individual applicant matched.

Categorical and advanced are different starts. A categorical program includes the clinical year; an advanced position begins at PGY-2 and requires a separate clinical-year plan.

Ranking data need context. NRMP applicant counts can include anyone who ranked at least one program in that specialty, even when it was not their first choice.

Applicant type is descriptive. These totals show who filled positions nationally. They do not capture program-specific screening, visa policy, clinical experience, or individual strengths.

PD + resident perspectives

What helps an application.

Recent national program-leadership data, paired with practical advice from IR residents, PDs, and APDs.

BEFORE THE INTERVIEW

Pass, score, signal.

In a 2026 survey of 85 DR and integrated IR program-leadership respondents, 90.6% highly considered or required a first-attempt Step 1/Level 1 pass, 88.2% did so for Step 2/Level 2 scores, and 87.1% for preference signals.

WHEN PROGRAMS RANK

The interview matters most.

Overall interview impression was highly considered or essential for ranking by 94.1% of respondents and was selected among the most significant ranking factors by 91.8%.

SHOWING FIT

Rotations can carry weight.

Visiting rotations positively influenced ranking for 77.6% of respondents. SIR resident guidance recommends choosing them deliberately for fit, geography, cost, and the chance to contribute meaningfully.

AFTER THE INTERVIEW

Letters of intent are limited.

Only 35.3% reported ranking an applicant higher because of a letter of intent. Use one only when it is genuine, specific, and consistent with program instructions.

These percentages describe one national survey and are not universal screening rules. Programs use different filters, priorities, and holistic-review processes.

Interview format · 2026–27

DR and IR guidance differs.

Do not assume that every radiology interview will use the same format this season.

DIAGNOSTIC RADIOLOGY · APDR

Virtual interviews are the preferred best practice.

APDR supports virtual interviews whenever possible. If a program offers in-person interviews, applicants should also have an equitable virtual option, and optional visits should not affect ranking.

Read APDR guidance ↗︎
INTERVENTIONAL RADIOLOGY · APDIR

In-person interviews are recommended for IR.

For 2026–27, APDIR recommends in-person interviews because applicants are evaluating small teams, procedural culture, and physical infrastructure. Virtual interviews and hybrid formats remain fully acceptable.

Details applicants often miss

Build the complete Match plan.

Radiology applications may involve separate training years, linked rank lists, travel, and contingency planning.

POSITION TYPE

An advanced radiology position does not include PGY-1.

Advanced positions begin at PGY-2 after required preliminary training. Categorical positions include the clinical year. Confirm the type of every code before applying and ranking.

Review NRMP position types ↗︎
RANKING

Link preliminary programs with supplemental rank lists.

If you rank advanced programs, a supplemental rank order list can link preferred preliminary or transitional-year programs to each advanced choice.

COUPLES MATCH

Build program pairs, not two independent lists.

Couples match to the highest preferred pair where both partners can be placed. Advanced-program supplemental lists are still considered individually.

PGY-1 planning

Choose the clinical year deliberately.

A transitional year, preliminary medicine year, or preliminary surgery year can each be compatible with an advanced radiology position when it meets the receiving program’s requirements.

Check current ACGME PGY-1 guidance ↗︎
TRANSITIONAL YEAR

Broad, rotating clinical training

A TY intentionally spans several disciplines. Current ACGME standards require at least 24 weeks of fundamental clinical skills, including inpatient, emergency, and ambulatory experience. Elective flexibility can be useful, but schedules and call intensity vary widely.

Review TY standards ↗︎
PRELIMINARY MEDICINE

Medicine-centered internship

Expect substantial ward-based internal medicine, with ICU, ambulatory, emergency, and subspecialty experiences varying by program. This can strengthen acute-care reasoning, consultation skills, and management of the comorbidities seen throughout radiology and IR.

Review IM standards ↗︎
PRELIMINARY SURGERY

Surgery-centered internship

Expect perioperative care, surgical wards and consults, with ICU, emergency, operating-room, and procedural exposure depending on the program. It can build comfort with postoperative complications and procedural teams, but workload and elective time are program-specific.

Review surgery standards ↗︎

What radiology applicants must verify

Eligibility first. Confirm in writing that the PGY-1 curriculum satisfies every advanced DR or integrated IR program you may rank. Do not rely on the internship’s title alone.

Direct patient care matters. Current ACGME radiology requirements emphasize fundamental clinical skills and limit radiology or IR electives; those electives must be active clinical experiences, not observerships.

Compare the real schedule. Ask for the block diagram, night and weekend structure, ICU and emergency exposure, elective rules, vacation timing, patient caps, and ancillary support.

Protect the transition. Compare location, housing, salary, benefits, onboarding, licensing, visa support, and whether orientation ends before your PGY-2 radiology start date.
