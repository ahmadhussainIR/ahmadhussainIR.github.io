For medical students

A practical IR applicant guide.

Concrete actions that build genuine exposure, useful skills, and a stronger residency application.

  1. 01

    See the full clinical workflow

    Shadow consults, clinic, procedures, recovery, and follow-up—not only the procedure room. Keep a short log of cases, questions, and what you learned about patient selection and longitudinal care.

  2. 02

    Find one dependable mentor

    Ask for a brief meeting with a specific goal. Bring a concise CV, respect their time, complete small tasks reliably, and send focused updates. Consistency is more valuable than collecting many superficial contacts.

  3. 03

    Choose a finishable project

    Define one clinical question, a feasible dataset, your exact role, and a realistic endpoint such as an abstract or manuscript. Learn literature review, data cleaning, basic statistics, and clear scientific writing along the way.

  4. 04

    Plan rotations and letters early

    Complete DR and IR exposure before applications when possible. On rotations, be prepared, teachable, and helpful. Request letters from physicians who supervised you directly and can describe your judgment, teamwork, and growth.

  5. 05

    Build a program comparison sheet

    Track integrated IR and DR/ESIR options, preliminary-year structure, geography, visa and IMG eligibility, case mix, mentorship, research support, interview format, and questions to ask at each open house.

  6. 06

    Prepare your application story

    Be ready to explain why radiology, why IR, how you work on a team, what you learned from a setback, and one project in depth. Use specific experiences instead of generic claims.
